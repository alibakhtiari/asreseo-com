## Introduction
SEO (Search Engine Optimization) is the process of improving a website's visibility in organic search results. In 2024, SEO is especially important because:

- Most online experiences begin with a search
- A large share of website traffic comes from organic search
- Users mostly check only the first page of Google

### Why SEO Matters

1. **Free traffic growth**: No need to pay for advertising
2. **Trust and credibility**: Users trust organic results more
3. **High ROI**: SEO is one of the best digital marketing channels

## Keyword Research
Keywords are the foundation of every successful SEO strategy. Without proper keyword research, all your efforts will go to waste.

### Keyword Research Steps

1. **Identify core topics**
   - List topics related to your business
   - Review customer questions
   - Analyze competitors

2. **Use research tools**
   - Google Keyword Planner
   - Ahrefs
   - SEMrush
   - Ubersuggest

3. **Analyze keywords**
   - Search volume
   - Competition difficulty
   - Relevance to your business

### Key Tips for Choosing Keywords

- **Long-tail keywords**: Three-word and longer queries make up a large share of searches
- **Search intent**: Choose keywords that match what users actually want
- **Balanced competition**: A mix of high-volume and low-competition keywords

## Content Optimization
Content is the heart of SEO. Google prioritizes high-quality, helpful, and comprehensive content.

### Principles of SEO-Friendly Content

1. **Quality over quantity**: One excellent piece beats 10 weak ones
2. **Answer user questions**: Content should solve users' problems
3. **Natural keyword use**: Don't stuff keywords

### Optimal Content Structure

```
Main title (H1)
├── Introduction
├── First subheading (H2)
│   ├── First section content
│   └── Inner subheading (H3)
├── Second subheading (H2)
└── Conclusion
```

### Key Content Writing Tips

- **Right length**: Long content (over 1,500 words) usually ranks better
- **High readability**: Short sentences and concise paragraphs
- **Use images**: Optimized images raise content quality

## [Technical SEO](/services/seo/technical-onpage/)
Technical SEO is your website's infrastructure for search engines.

### Key Technical SEO Factors

1. **Loading speed**
   - Fast sites rank better
   - Goal: under 3 seconds
   - Testing tools: Google PageSpeed Insights

2. **Mobile-friendliness**
   - Most searches happen on mobile
   - Responsive design is essential

3. **HTTPS**
   - An SSL certificate is mandatory
   - A Google ranking factor

### Technical Optimization Tips

- **XML Sitemap**: A guide for search engines to index your site
- **robots.txt**: Control search bot access
- **URL structure**: Simple, descriptive addresses

## Link Building
External links remain one of Google's most important ranking factors.

### Link Building Strategies

1. **Link-worthy content**
   - Comprehensive guides
   - Useful infographics
   - Original research

2. **Digital PR**
   - Connect with bloggers
   - Appear on podcasts
   - Write guest articles

3. **Internal links**
   - Connect related pages
   - Distribute link equity

### Key Link Building Tips

- **Quality over quantity**: 10 quality links beat 100 poor ones
- **Source diversity**: Links from different websites
- **Stay natural**: Avoid buying links

## Measurement and Analysis
Without measurement, you can't evaluate SEO success.

### Key SEO KPIs

1. **Keyword rankings**
   - Position in search results
   - Ranking changes over time

2. **Organic traffic**
   - Visits from search
   - Monthly traffic growth

3. **Click-through rate (CTR)**
   - Percentage of clicks on results
   - Optimizing titles and descriptions

### Essential Tools

- **Google Analytics**: Website traffic analysis
- **Google Search Console**: Performance in Google search
- **Google PageSpeed Insights**: Website speed

## September 2026 Update: What Has Changed?

This guide was written in January 2024 for beginners. Its five principles (keyword research, content optimization, technical SEO, link building, and measurement) are still the foundation. But three fundamental shifts have happened that every beginner should know from day one.

### 1. The Goal Is No Longer Just "Rankings" — It's "Being Cited in Answers"

The biggest shift between 2024 and 2026 is that a significant share of searches are answered right on the results page. BrightEdge data shows Google's AI answers appear on roughly **48% of tracked queries**, up from about 30% a year earlier (BrightEdge, 2026: https://web.archive.org/web/20260805203541/https://www.brightedge.com/resources/weekly-ai-search-insights/ai-overviews-one-year-presence-size-citing), and in their presence the average click-through rate of the #1 result drops by about **58%** (Ahrefs, February 2026: https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/).

That doesn't mean SEO is over. Pages cited inside these answers earn far more clicks per impression and convert better. So alongside this guide's principles, you need one new skill: **writing to be cited**.

### 2. Generic Content Is No Longer Enough

Covering a topic with a few standard paragraphs used to be enough to rank. Today, language models easily combine and reproduce that same content from multiple sources. Real value lies in things that can't be reproduced:

- Your own first-hand data and statistics
- Practical experience and real project outcomes
- Case studies with specific numbers and timelines
- Clear expert opinions backed by reasoning

### 3. Technical Benchmarks Have Been Updated

If you're familiar with Core Web Vitals, remember one key change: since March 2024, **INP (Interaction to Next Paint)** has replaced **FID (First Input Delay)**. Today, instead of the delay of the first interaction, the page's overall responsiveness to user interactions is measured.

Alongside that, the three core metrics remain the same: **LCP** (loading speed of the largest element), **CLS** (visual stability), and **INP** (responsiveness to interaction).

### Where Should You Start?

If you're just starting out, here is our recommended path for 2026:

1. Apply this guide's principles as-is; none of them are obsolete.
2. For each page, determine whether it's exposed to AI answers (question-based and comparison queries carry the most risk).
3. On informational pages, add direct answers and extractable structure (tables, lists, short definitions).
4. Shift your success metric from rankings to "clicks per impression."
